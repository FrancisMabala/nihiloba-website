## 3. Information and sources

We obtain information you enter on the web, send to SHIDA through WhatsApp or supply to support; information generated by your activities; and relevant information supplied by the counterparty or an authorized Organization member.

- Account and onboarding: your WhatsApp telephone number and sender identifier, name and other profile fields you provide, language, account type choice, onboarding progress, verification state, membership, roles and permissions.
- Legal records: document/version, acceptance time, person accepting, language, channel and applicable Organization context. A Privacy Policy acknowledgement is recorded separately in meaning from optional consent.
- Public content: listings, business contact information you choose to publish, descriptions, location at the displayed level, photos and permitted public reviews. External job discovery may import publicly available job advertisements and source links; this is not an import of private candidate profiles.
- Private activities: applications and supported attachments, order items and amounts, booking/visit/service requests, correspondence, delivery or service address where needed, status changes and records entered by authorized managers. Payment method/status and receipts may be recorded without SHIDA handling the payment itself.
- Communications: messages sent to SHIDA, the relevant activity references, delivery/status information and support or moderation exchanges. SHIDA receives the contents you send to its business account; do not assume those messages are inaccessible to the operator.
- Technical/security information: request time, device/browser information available in requests, IP information in infrastructure logs, session and verification identifiers, security events, permissions/audit history and limited usage events where enabled. Some application counters use hashed identifiers; hashing does not necessarily make information anonymous.

Do not send unnecessary identity documents, health information, criminal records, children's information or other sensitive details in free text, pictures or attachments. A free-text field is not a request for those details.

## 4. Purposes and legal grounds

Where the GDPR applies, the purpose/basis allocation is as follows. This allocation applies only to the processing described here; a different purpose needs its own assessment and information.

- Creating and administering the Personal service you request, verifying access, completing onboarding, handling your requested activity and delivering essential service messages: performance of the contract with you or steps you request before entering it, limited to what is objectively necessary.
- Preventing fraud, investigating misuse, protecting accounts and maintaining reliable service: the operator's legitimate interests in safe service operation, subject to a documented necessity and rights-balancing assessment. A legal obligation is used only when a specific applicable obligation actually requires the processing.

- Keeping legal acceptance and necessary dispute evidence: applicable legal obligations where identified, or the legitimate interest in demonstrating compliance and defending claims, with limited access and a justified retention period.
- Optional marketing or non-essential device tracking: prior consent where required. Declining these optional uses must not prevent access to unrelated core services. Service measurement requires its own basis and assessment; calling it analytics does not make it necessary.

The DRC Code's processing and disclosure conditions must be satisfied separately. In particular, its consent/legal-obligation provisions cannot simply be replaced by a GDPR contractual or legitimate-interest basis. Any specific consent required by applicable law must be sought for its stated purpose. You may contact the operator to withdraw consent. Required regulatory formalities are not replaced by this notice. Accepting terms or acknowledging this notice is not a general authorization for all uses or international transfers.

## 9. Cookies and browser storage

SHIDA uses session cookies for protected access and browser storage for functions such as cross-tab sign-in notifications, the Personal/Business onboarding choice, restaurant basket continuity and a selected restaurant workspace preference. Browser storage may also be used to avoid counting repeated job views. These functions must be assessed individually; an analytics purpose is not automatically exempt from consent.

Session storage normally lasts for the tab session, although browsers can restore sessions. Local storage persists until cleared or removed by the application; cookies have their configured expiry. Clearing necessary storage may sign you out or remove saved choices. Necessary access and requested-service storage is used for the purposes above. Job-view deduplication storage is not needed to create an account. Browser settings let you clear stored data; this can remove saved preferences or sessions.

## 10. How long information is kept

We must keep identifiable information only for its justified purpose and duration, then erase or genuinely anonymize it unless a specific lawful preservation need applies. Expiry of access or hiding a record is not the same as physical deletion.

The reviewed implementation provides the following technical rules, subject to verification of production configuration and scheduled execution:

- Verification challenges have a short validity capped at five minutes; restricted onboarding sessions default to 30 minutes, authenticated sessions to seven days and cross-service handoffs to 60 seconds. These are access-validity periods, not database-deletion guarantees.
- Inactive onboarding drafts expire after 30 days. Draft expiry prevents continuing the expired draft; it does not by itself erase all stored draft values. You can request erasure through the contact above.
- Raw platform analytics older than 90 days are eligible for cleanup, with an account-deletion path. WhatsApp processed-event replay records have a separate 90-day cleanup rule. This does not establish a 90-day lifetime for WhatsApp message content or every job-analytics record.
- Restaurant delivery-destination handling has a 24-hour post-terminal-state rule and private order history a 12-month rule. Inventory records have a 24-UTC-month policy, but physical inventory deletion is not demonstrated as enabled. Holds and access restrictions must be checked separately.
- Some anti-replay/erasure-barrier digests remain without a defined deletion deadline in the reviewed analytics design. They may still be personal data. These security digests are retained to prevent replay and recreation of erased events; the current implementation has no fixed automatic deletion deadline for them. Separate external-job analytics does not inherit the platform analytics cleanup period.

Account and profile information is retained while the account is used to provide the service. Applications, messages, private files, marketplace records and support exchanges are retained for the requested activity and necessary follow-up; there is no single verified automatic deletion period across these features. On closure or an erasure request, the operator must assess and remove information no longer needed, subject to specific legal recordkeeping or claims requirements. Acceptance and audit evidence is retained only to the extent justified by those requirements. Contact us for the applicable category and any preservation reason; a technical absence of automatic cleanup is not a legal reason to refuse erasure.

Legal acceptance history must not be rewritten. Any continued retention must nevertheless have a documented basis and limited duration. A particular dispute or statutory obligation may require a limited preservation hold; we will explain applicable restrictions when responding to a request. The mere existence of a historic paid record must not be treated as an automatic refusal of all erasure rights.

## 11. Security and deletion limits

The reviewed design uses access permissions, browser-bound verification, session protections, private-document authorization and controls intended to keep secrets out of logs. No system can promise absolute security. Some deletion, legacy-file and recovery procedures require operator intervention; the availability of code alone does not prove every historical copy has been removed.

Deleting information from the active service may leave restricted backup copies until the verified backup cycle expires; restoration must reapply erasure decisions. The documented Render database recovery window is seven days. Independent encrypted AWS backup tiers have lifecycle periods of 14, 56 and 93 days, with versioning and a 14-day protection lock; provider lifecycle execution can add processing delay. Cloudinary media backups are separate and are not covered by the database periods. Before restoring service, the operator must reconcile later erasure and access-revocation decisions. Ask the operator about a specific retained backup or media copy. Recipients may independently retain information under their own lawful obligations. We will not describe pseudonymized information as anonymous merely because a direct name or number has been removed.
